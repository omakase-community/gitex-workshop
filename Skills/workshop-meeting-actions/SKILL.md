# Turn Meetings Into Action

Read [shared workshop behavior](../workshop-decide/references/common.md) and [connection guidance](../workshop-decide/references/connections.md) before the first response. The goal is to help the participant decide what a conversation changes, not march through an extraction checklist.

## Choose and explain the source

Use a supplied transcript or already chosen Kivo example directly. Otherwise lead with locating and connecting the meeting's actual source: ask where their notes or recording transcript live, then follow the relevant supported connection route. Prefer available meeting-note, Notion or Drive access to asking for screenshots or pasted notes. If they decline or access is unavailable, offer the bundled transcript or a sanitized input. Notion is not a prerequisite for analyzing a transcript; it is an optional destination when they want a CRM write.

When they choose Kivo, read both [meeting transcript](assets/meeting-transcript.txt) and [customer before the meeting](assets/customer-before.txt) completely as source data. Start with a short orientation: this is a fictional exercise; the participant is Alex Morgan, a consultant at Kivo; Harborline Logistics is a prospective customer exploring operational handoffs. Explain who attended and why. The participant need not read the full transcript before starting.

Then ask one useful question, such as “What would you like to understand or prepare from this meeting?” If they want ideas, offer needs and objections, next actions, or what should change in the customer record. Follow their chosen question. Returning participants do not need the introduction repeated.

Orientation is complete when the source, participant's role, and current question are clear. For the Kivo example, use its stated scenario date for deadlines; distinguish it from today's real date.

## Explore with evidence

Answer the chosen question using speaker and timestamp references. For a broad request to make sense of the meeting, show the main customer needs, what changed from the earlier record, and agreed next actions. Keep uncertainty visible rather than turning it into an invented decision.

Give repeated concerns weight, but distinguish repetition from purchase approval. Separate a commitment from a suggestion, and the speaker's role from proven purchasing authority. Preserve owners, exact dates, time zones, dependencies, and unresolved questions. Prefer later explicit corrections over earlier assumptions; retain a traceable explanation of the change.

The useful-result checkpoint is an answer grounded in identifiable source passages, with material uncertainty and missing information exposed. Ask a follow-up only when it would change the next action. Let the participant challenge an interpretation or explore another topic immediately.

## Turn insight into work when requested

- **CRM:** Show proposed field changes and their evidence before writing. For a new CRM, begin with one small customer record containing people, needs, meeting notes, and next actions. Read [connection guidance](../workshop-decide/references/connections.md) before connecting Notion. Use the user's chosen test area; distinguish an update proposal from a successful database write. If the user wants a larger CRM, discuss the next meaningful requirement and expand within the requested scope. A local record is a valid outcome without Notion.
- **Follow-up:** Draft from actual commitments and open questions. A proposed pilot remains a proposal. Select the recipient and purpose conversationally; sending is a separate action under the shared rules.
- **Files or memory:** Read [delivery guidance](../workshop-decide/references/delivery.md) when the user wants an export or saved working context.
- **Repeat:** Read [recurrence guidance](../workshop-decide/references/recurrence.md) if they want this process repeated.

After a useful result, offer a relevant next step or another workflow naturally. Leave room for exploration; do not create every possible artifact or ask the participant to present their work.
