KIVO - FICTIONAL WORKSHOP COMPANY

Everything in this company brief is invented for the workshop. Kivo is not the participant's employer unless they explicitly choose this scenario. Addresses ending in .example cannot receive mail.

Company: Kivo
Location: United States; consulting clients across the US
Team: 32 people
Business: Technology consulting for growing B2B logistics businesses

Kivo helps operations, technology, and sales teams connect the systems they already use. Its projects include CRM-to-operations integration, reducing repeated data entry, reliable handoffs between sales and operations, and practical workflow automation. It does not assume every client needs a replacement CRM.

Typical buyers: Operations leaders who own a broken handoff, technology leaders responsible for integration and security, and sales operations leaders who need reliable customer information. Those people may have different priorities and different authority to approve a project.

Working ICP hypothesis: US logistics companies with roughly 100-1,000 employees, especially when growth exposes manual handoffs or disconnected customer and operations information. This is a starting hypothesis for exploration, not a verified market finding or a fixed rule for the participant's own company.

Participant role in Kivo examples: Alex Morgan, a Kivo consultant who works with prospects, runs discovery conversations, coordinates with the technical team, and follows up on commitments. Alex writes concise, specific, low-pressure business emails. Email: alex@kivo.example.

Featured customer: Harborline Logistics, a fictional 340-person US logistics business. Maya Chen is VP of Operations, Daniel Reed leads IT, and Lena Ortiz manages Sales Operations. They are exploring how to improve the handoff from a won opportunity to an operational customer setup. Interest in a workflow pilot is not approval for a full CRM replacement.

Time convention: Example inbox planning uses Monday, September 7, 2026 at 09:00 America/New_York. The meeting took place Thursday, September 3. Treat this as a frozen scenario clock, not the real present. The scenario includes customer-specific working dates; do not infer availability from a generic holiday calendar.

Using Kivo for research: Kivo can be a fictional seller while Codex researches real prospect companies on the public web. Keep those real findings separate from the clearly labeled fictional offline company cards.
