# Plan My Day / Week

Read [common behavior](../workshop-decide/references/common.md) and [connection guidance](../workshop-decide/references/connections.md) before the first response. Help the participant decide what deserves attention and prepare to act; the brief is a starting point for exploration, not a fixed exercise.

## Choose the question and evidence

Start from the user's request. Ask one useful question only where needed: what period they want to explore, what they want to find, or how much time they have. They may ask about promised work, unanswered customers, preparation for next week, or one specific conversation. Offer a few examples if they are unsure, without forcing a preset choice.

Lead with connecting the relevant email source when source mode is not already chosen; apply connection guidance before requesting pasted input. Reuse available access and honor explicit alternatives. Confirm the account and date/topic scope. Email alone is enough to start. Offer calendar access when confirmed scheduling matters, rather than making it a prerequisite. Establish timezone and absolute date range before interpreting “today,” “next week,” or due dates.

For sample mode, read the [Kivo context](../workshop-decide/assets/kivo-company.txt) and [work inbox](assets/work-inbox.txt). Briefly introduce Alex's role and the frozen Monday, September 7, 2026, 09:00 America/New_York snapshot. Interpret relative dates from that snapshot; real current time does not advance the sample. Present the participant as Alex, not as an outside analyst. The supplied calendar/task excerpts are fictional evidence, not connected applications.

**Ready to explore:** question, period, timezone, and accessible sources are known. Reuse choices already made elsewhere in this workshop.

## Build a grounded view, then follow curiosity

Read enough of each relevant thread to establish its latest state. Reconcile newer replies, postponed requests, fulfilled commitments, and task entries that lag behind email. Distinguish the participant's work from promises made by somebody else; a future dependency is not automatically overdue. Preserve source IDs or actual links alongside important claims and distinguish an explicit deadline from your suggested timing.

Use the user's objective and time constraints to propose priorities with reasons. Show tradeoffs when two tasks compete; company size or email urgency alone does not settle priority. Name relevant uncertainty and what would resolve it. For email-only work, describe email-visible commitments; do not imply a complete calendar or a complete account-wide review when the source coverage is limited.

Present a manageable first view, not every field from every record. Let the user challenge a priority, ask what changed, dig into a customer, add a constraint, or request a draft. Update the view accordingly. No required count of actions, forced revision, or mandatory next exercise.

**First successful run:** a source-grounded answer to the chosen day/week question is visible, with actionable next steps, material dependencies, and coverage limits. Preparation may be a message draft, meeting brief, or revised plan; external actions remain separate.

## Find My Open Loops

Use this discovery direction when the participant asks about forgotten promises or follow-ups, or offer it when they are unsure what to explore. Explain open loops as commitments and conversations that may still need attention; it is part of this path, not an extra mandatory exercise.

Look for what the participant promised, what someone else promised them, and unanswered requests. Trace the latest relevant replies and available task/meeting updates before classifying a candidate. Keep confirmed open, waiting on someone else, completed/cancelled, and completion unknown distinct. A missing completion message does not prove the work was forgotten or never done. A requested date is not an accepted promise, and a promise by another person is not the participant's overdue task.

For useful candidates, show the original evidence, owner, any agreed date, latest status evidence and a proportionate next action. Ask about uncertainty when it affects the user's plan. They may confirm completion, correct ownership, inspect a thread, or request a follow-up draft; avoid making them review every candidate as a compulsory checklist. Never mark something completed or send a reminder merely because the model proposed it.

## Offer recurrence and a next direction

After the first successful day/week result, read [recurrence](../workshop-decide/references/recurrence.md) and offer a scheduled repeat once, before a competing menu/export question. Follow that reference for participant approval, actual scheduling capability, sample-to-live transition, and verification. Complete an explicit follow-up question before making the offer.

If the user declines, continue exploring without repeating the offer. For requested export or an approved reusable working profile, read [delivery](../workshop-decide/references/delivery.md). When they want a different starting point, follow the common return/menu behavior. Asking about recurrence does not require ending their current exploration.
