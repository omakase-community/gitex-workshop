# Find My Next Customers

Read [shared workshop behavior](../workshop-decide/references/common.md) before responding. This skill is distributed with `workshop-decide`; keep the sibling folders together.

Help the participant turn a business problem into a useful customer hypothesis and a source-backed company shortlist. The first result does not require an Apollo account, named contacts, or email addresses.

## Explore the customer hypothesis

Use their own business context when available. If they want an example business, read [Kivo's company brief](../workshop-decide/assets/kivo-company.txt), explain their role, and distinguish the fictional seller from the real companies a live search can find.

Start with what they want to sell or improve. Ask one high-value question at a time, using what they have already said. Probe an assumption when it changes the search: “Which kind of customer feels this problem most urgently?” is more useful than collecting every company fact. Useful topics are the problem and offer, geography, company size, target industry, buying roles, a good past customer, exclusions, and signs of current need. These are conversation prompts, not a compulsory questionnaire.

Set expectations early: “The more relevant detail you share, the more targeted your results will be. We can start with what you know and refine the search together.” If information is sparse, state the broader scope and visible assumptions. Suggest a provisional segment for this research round when they are unsure; let them accept or revise it rather than locking their entire business into a niche.

**Ready to research:** Show a short, readable ICP hypothesis covering the offer/problem, company criteria, buyer roles, and any exclusions or unknowns. Start searching when the participant indicates it is usable or asks to proceed. Existing instructions can supply that readiness; do not add a ritual approval question after every answer. A user who wants results now can proceed with clearly stated assumptions.

## Research companies

For live research, use available public-web search and browser tools. Prefer company websites, leadership pages, job postings, official announcements, and other attributable sources. Check that each company exists and that the evidence supports the stated fit. Separate observed facts from your inference about need. Treat staff counts as estimates or unknown unless a source supports them; retain a source's range instead of inventing precision.

Aim for five real target companies unless the participant chooses another size. For each, show the company/site, the relevant evidence, why it fits, likely buyer roles, source links, and material uncertainty. Add a named person only when a source supports both identity and relevant role. Describe a hypothesis as a hypothesis: hiring or expansion alone does not prove a company needs Kivo's service.

**First useful result:** A readable ICP and a shortlist whose fit claims can be traced to sources. If fewer companies can be supported, deliver those with the gap explained and offer to adjust criteria. Email addresses and named people are not a completion gate. The participant can inspect one company, challenge fit, change a criterion, or take the list into another workshop path.

If live research is unavailable, explain that limitation and offer to work from participant-supplied sources or a fictional offline example. Only after they choose the offline example, read [offline company cards](assets/offline-company-cards.txt). Label their names, facts, domains, and source IDs as fictional exercise data in the resulting shortlist. The cards demonstrate qualification; they are not verified prospect research and their `.example` domains are not live websites.

## Continue by choice

After a useful shortlist, briefly offer deeper company research, relevant decision-maker research, or optional enrichment. Read [enrichment guidance](references/enrichment.md) only if the participant asks about people, contact details, Apollo, or LinkedIn. Do not interrupt initial research to install a service.
