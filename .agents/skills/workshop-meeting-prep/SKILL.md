# Get Ready for My Next Meeting

At entry, read [shared workshop behavior](../workshop-decide/references/common.md) and [connection guidance](../workshop-decide/references/connections.md) before the first source request. Help the participant prepare for the conversation they actually need, not produce a generic meeting summary.

## Choose the meeting and purpose

For the participant's own meeting, begin with the relevant connection:

- If the meeting is not identified, suggest connecting their calendar to find it. Ask the calendar provider only if unknown; reuse available access and narrow the search to a useful period.
- If they already identified the meeting, work from that information and suggest connecting email for its conversation history. A calendar connection is unnecessary merely to repeat an established date or title.
- Honor an explicit choice of examples, supplied material, or no connection. Use the shared fallback when setup is unavailable. Explain gaps rather than making every account a prerequisite.

Establish the selected meeting, its date/time zone, the participant's role, and desired outcome conversationally. Ask one useful question at a time, skipping known answers. “What would make this meeting a success for you?” is a helpful opening once the meeting is clear. If they are unsure, propose an outcome from the invitation and ask what they think. A specific user goal already answers this question.

Move into preparation when the meeting and desired outcome are clear enough to guide retrieval. An event title alone need not determine the participant's agenda.

## Build the relevant context

Search the selected account's relevant email threads using the company, attendees, subject, and agreed time range. Read the latest replies as well as the original request so changed dates, completed work, or new concerns are reflected. Add an already connected Notion page or Slack thread only when it helps answer a preparation question; explain the missing context before proposing an additional connection. Public company research is optional, not a substitute for the actual conversation.

Separate what sources establish from what the participant wants to achieve. Bring forward relevant prior decisions, concerns, commitments and dependencies, with source links or sample IDs. Preserve who owes what to whom, exact deadlines, and uncertain completion status. “No completion found in the searched material” is not proof the work was never done. A possible concern or likely question from another attendee is a hypothesis, not their confirmed agenda.

Use this context to create a brief fitted to the participant's purpose: what matters going in, what needs resolution, useful questions and any preparation still needed. An agenda can help, but is a proposal rather than a mandatory format. Match its ambition to the actual meeting duration and authority of the attendees. The checkpoint is a usable preparation brief with traceable claims and important gaps visible, not a target number of questions or sections.

## Explore the result

Invite a neutral reaction, such as “How does this look?” Leave room to focus on an attendee's concern, adjust the goal, or explore a source. Further revision is optional; do not imply the first result must be wrong. Offer rehearsal only when it fits the participant's interest. In a rehearsal, label the counterpart's responses as simulated possibilities and ground the role in known context.

Preparation does not authorize changing an invitation, contacting attendees, or sending materials. Follow the shared action rules if the participant asks for those actions. Read [delivery](../workshop-decide/references/delivery.md) for requested exports or saved context, and [recurrence](../workshop-decide/references/recurrence.md) for repeated preparation. Return to another workflow at a natural stopping point or whenever requested.
