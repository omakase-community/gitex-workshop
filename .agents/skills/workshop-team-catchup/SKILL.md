# Catch Up With My Team

Help the participant understand what changed and what matters to them, with the conversation behind each conclusion.

## Connect and choose a useful scope

At entry, read the shared [conversation and source rules](../workshop-decide/references/common.md) and [connection procedure](../workshop-decide/references/connections.md) before the first participant-facing action. Offer the relevant Slack connection as the default path before requesting pasted messages or screenshots. Reuse a connected account; honor an explicit example choice or refusal without another connection pitch.

If context is missing, explain the benefit and ask one useful question: which team or channel would they like to catch up with? Establish the workspace, channel, period, and timezone before searching private conversations; carry forward choices already made. Start with one channel unless the participant asks for broader coverage. Ask what matters to them when that will make the reading more useful, rather than running a fixed intake.

For examples, read [slack-channel.txt](assets/slack-channel.txt). Introduce the participant as Alex Morgan, a consultant at fictional Kivo, following the internal delivery-improvements channel. Its snapshot is Monday, September 7, 2026, 09:00 America/New_York, not the real current date.

## Follow the changes

Read relevant threads, including later replies, rather than treating search snippets as final decisions. Track a subject from its earlier state to the latest supported state: what was decided, what changed, what was resolved, what remains uncertain, and why it matters to the participant. A request, proposed change, accepted decision, and completed action have different statuses. Check subsequent context before calling an issue unresolved or a commitment overdue; no completion message means completion is unverified, not necessarily that the work was missed.

Choose a fair comparison with the participant's goal. Label a partial current week versus a complete previous week explicitly, or compare equivalent elapsed periods if the available history supports that comparison. Keep the period boundaries visible. Message counts, posting frequency, and quieter channels are not evidence of individual productivity or team performance.

Support actionable findings with Slack links or sample message IDs and the relevant speaker/date. State coverage limits: a selected channel cannot establish everything a company did, and missing access or unread thread replies constrain the conclusion. Separate useful interpretation from the source's own claim.

Give a first useful catch-up in chat, organized around the participant's question rather than a mandatory report template. They should be able to tell what changed, what may need their attention, and which items are uncertain without rereading every message.

## Keep the conversation open

A useful result is a place to react, not a compulsory menu hand-off. An optional neutral invitation such as "What do you think? We can look more closely at any of these" can lead into one decision's history, a blocker, another period, or a draft follow-up. Do not require critique, a second exercise, or a channel post before they can finish or switch paths.

Posting, messaging colleagues, or changing external tasks requires authorization for the destination and content. An analysis is not a posted Slack update. For requested exports or recovery, read [delivery](../workshop-decide/references/delivery.md). For requested recurring catch-ups, read [recurrence](../workshop-decide/references/recurrence.md); use its verified scheduling or guided setup route.
